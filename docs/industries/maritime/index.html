Port call readiness and exception coordination

Reconcile vessel arrival estimates, berth constraints, cargo readiness, and agency documents for port-operator decisions.

Explore in the architecture studio

The business situation

Illustrative reference architecture. Validate requirements for your organization.

Illustrative architecture: A shipping line prepares a port call while arrival estimates, terminal readiness, pilot availability, and cargo documents change independently. Operations staff need a coherent readiness picture. Agents support coordination and document checks; masters, pilots, terminal operators, and authorized officials retain navigation and clearance authority.

Problem

Estimated arrival is not a berthing commitment, and a complete document packet is not official clearance. Conflicting timezones and late cargo updates can make a schedule appear feasible when an essential service remains unconfirmed.

Why a multi-agent system?

Arrival, terminal, document, and service-coordination specialists inspect separate interfaces. A durable port-call workflow tracks dependencies and authoritative acknowledgements without granting agents control over navigation or official declarations.

Intended outcome

The intended result is a reviewable operational decision with source evidence and explicit ownership. Measure coordination clarity and unresolved dependency age alongside port-call performance. Berthing and cargo outcomes depend on physical operations and authorized decisions; a complete agent packet does not itself establish operational or regulatory readiness.

Runtime flow

  1. 1

    Open port-call case

    Bind vessel, voyage, port, terminal, and responsible operator using governed identifiers and explicit timezones.

  2. 2

    Reconcile arrival evidence

    Compare estimate revisions and report age; retain uncertainty instead of converting a predicted time into a commitment.

  3. 3

    Gather readiness constraints

    Retrieve berth, cargo, pilot, tug, and agency dependencies from their authoritative owners.

  4. 4

    Check documentation

    Apply the configured document checklist and distinguish submitted, accepted, rejected, and pending official states.

  5. 5

    Prepare coordination options

    Show operators feasible sequences and unresolved confirmations without proposing navigational or clearance decisions.

  6. 6

    Record approved coordination

    Submit authorized booking or document requests with stable keys and track each external acknowledgement before declaring readiness.

Failure recovery

A local-time berth window is interpreted as UTC.

Detect: Timezone validation conflicts with terminal metadata and service booking intervals.

Recover: Block the schedule, normalize times with source timezone, and request operator review of affected bookings.

Documents are submitted but official acceptance is pending.

Detect: Acknowledgement state lacks a valid acceptance reference.

Recover: Keep clearance pending and route follow-up to the authorized agent instead of reporting the vessel cleared.

Security & decision boundaries

  • Keep navigation, vessel safety, and official clearance decisions outside agent authority.
  • Attach timezone, source, and revision to every arrival and service-window timestamp.
  • Distinguish document completeness from submission and official acceptance.
  • Require operator approval for pilot, tug, berth-related requests, and agency correspondence.
  • Track service reservations and release expired options to avoid conflicting bookings.

Operational metrics

Measurement definitions, not promised improvements.

Readiness packet latency

Time from port-call update to operator-reviewable dependency and document status.

Confirmation completeness

Required services with current authoritative acknowledgements divided by required services for the approved plan.

Timestamp consistency

Audited schedule events with correct timezone and source revision divided by audited events.

Pending dependency age

Elapsed time of each unresolved readiness dependency, reported by owning organization.
