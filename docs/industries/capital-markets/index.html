Trade settlement break resolution

Investigate unmatched allocations and settlement instructions without granting agents trading or settlement authority.

Explore in the architecture studio

The business situation

Illustrative reference architecture. Validate requirements for your organization.

Illustrative architecture: A securities operations desk must resolve settlement breaks across broker allocations, custodian instructions, and internal books before market-specific cutoffs. The illustrative system prepares operational evidence for qualified staff; it does not provide investment advice or choose trades.

Problem

A trade can match economically while failing on account, instrument, or settlement-location identifiers. Messages arrive out of order and corrections supersede prior instructions. A plausible narrative that ignores a late amendment can cause an operator to approve the wrong repair.

Why a multi-agent system?

Specialists partition reconciliation by instrument identity, allocation economics, standing instructions, and exception history. Deterministic comparison services perform calculations; agents explain mismatches and retrieve context while a workflow tracks source versions and cutoffs.

Intended outcome

The intended result is a reviewable operational decision with source evidence and explicit ownership. Evaluate whether the desk spends less time assembling facts while maintaining correct repair decisions. Distinguish message acceptance, matching, and final settlement in dashboards so an intermediate success cannot hide unresolved exposure.

Architecture diagrams

Runtime flow

  1. 1

    Ingest break

    Deduplicate the mismatch event and attach trading date, settlement date, account scope, and market cutoff.

  2. 2

    Resolve identity

    Match instrument and counterparty identifiers through governed master data; unresolved mappings stop downstream repair generation.

  3. 3

    Reconcile economics

    Compare allocation quantities and amounts with deterministic tolerances configured for the instrument and market.

  4. 4

    Inspect instructions

    Read the trusted instruction version and message history; treat email attachments as evidence requiring authentication.

  5. 5

    Stage repair

    Present proposed field-level changes, source versions, unresolved risks, and remaining cutoff time to the authorized operator.

  6. 6

    Submit and monitor

    The gateway rechecks trade and instruction versions, sends an approved repair with a stable key, then tracks acknowledgement separately from settlement completion.

Failure recovery

A late allocation amendment arrives after operator approval.

Detect: Optimistic version checks find a different allocation revision before submission.

Recover: Cancel the staged repair and request approval of the new delta rather than merging silently.

Custodian status is unavailable near cutoff.

Detect: Gateway freshness and elapsed deadline checks classify the status as unknown.

Recover: Escalate to the desk's established contingency process and reconcile before any duplicate instruction.

Security & decision boundaries

  • Separate analyst identities from credentials that can submit settlement messages.
  • Authenticate instruction changes through the existing counterparty maintenance process.
  • Pin economic comparisons to trade and allocation versions with exact numeric precision.
  • Restrict each desk to authorized accounts, counterparties, and information barriers.
  • Record cutoff timezone and holiday calendar source; never infer them from model memory.

Operational metrics

Measurement definitions, not promised improvements.

Break preparation latency

Time from unmatched event to complete repair packet, reported by market and instrument class.

False mismatch rate

Reviewed alerts with no actionable discrepancy divided by all reviewed alerts.

Stale approval rejection

Repair submissions blocked by changed source versions divided by attempted approved submissions.

Unresolved cutoff exposure

Count and notional of unresolved breaks at desk-defined cutoff checkpoints, with currency conversion provenance.
