Field operations planning assistant

Combine field observations, weather, equipment, and agronomist-approved practices into reviewable farm work plans.

Explore in the architecture studio

The business situation

Illustrative reference architecture. Validate requirements for your organization.

Illustrative architecture: A grower cooperative coordinates irrigation inspections and field work across farms with uneven sensor coverage. Satellite imagery, soil readings, weather forecasts, and operator observations arrive at different resolutions. The system prepares field-specific evidence and scheduling options while agronomists and farm managers make crop and application decisions.

Problem

A coarse weather forecast or ambiguous image cannot establish conditions in every field. Unit mistakes, boundary errors, and stale sensor readings can turn a reasonable summary into an unsuitable work recommendation, particularly when farms share machinery and water access.

Why a multi-agent system?

Observation, weather, resource, and practice-retrieval specialists keep uncertain evidence separate. A scheduling engine applies manager-approved constraints; the agents explain missing information and propose inspections before high-consequence actions.

Intended outcome

The intended result is a reviewable operational decision with source evidence and explicit ownership. Measure useful inspections and planning effort before claiming changes in yield or resource use. Agronomic outcomes depend on local conditions and professional decisions, so retain those contextual variables when evaluating the workflow.

Runtime flow

  1. 1

    Register field concern

    Bind the observation to a farm and field polygon, retaining source resolution and any boundary uncertainty.

  2. 2

    Check observation quality

    Validate sensor units, calibration status, and capture time; label missing coverage rather than interpolating hidden certainty.

  3. 3

    Gather local context

    Retrieve crop stage, prior field work, access constraints, and agronomist-approved practices relevant to the task.

  4. 4

    Evaluate work windows

    Compare weather revisions, equipment availability, and shared resource constraints using declared planning assumptions.

  5. 5

    Review proposed inspection

    Present managers and agronomists with evidence, uncertainty, and a bounded task proposal before any operational commitment.

  6. 6

    Record approved work

    Publish the accepted task through the farm platform and capture operator observations to confirm or reject the original concern.

Failure recovery

A soil probe reports in a different unit than expected.

Detect: Schema validation detects a unit mismatch or impossible value range.

Recover: Reject the reading until the sensor mapping is corrected and show the field as lacking reliable measurement.

A field polygon overlaps a neighboring farm.

Detect: Boundary registry and observation footprint checks disagree on ownership.

Recover: Restrict retrieval and task generation until the responsible manager confirms the correct parcel boundary.

Security & decision boundaries

  • Validate geospatial ownership and farm access before exposing field-level observations.
  • Attach units, capture time, and spatial resolution to every sensor or imagery claim.
  • Keep agronomic prescriptions and chemical application decisions with qualified local professionals.
  • Require manager approval before dispatching machinery or reserving shared resources.
  • Expire weather-dependent plans and recheck conditions before field execution.

Operational metrics

Measurement definitions, not promised improvements.

Observation usability

Field concerns with sufficient validated local evidence divided by concerns evaluated.

Planning preparation time

Elapsed time from accepted concern to manager-reviewable inspection or work options.

Spatial attribution accuracy

Audited observations assigned to the correct field divided by audited observations.

Plan expiry rate

Approved tasks requiring revalidation because weather or access assumptions changed divided by approved tasks.

Shadow seasonal pilot

A bounded crop and region assess task usefulness without equipment control or application recommendations.

Approved task coordination

Shared-resource reservations begin only after weather-expiry and cross-farm access tests pass.
