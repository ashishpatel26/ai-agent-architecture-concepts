Editorial evidence and rights coordination

Assemble source verification, asset rights, and publication checks for editor-owned release decisions.

Explore in the architecture studio

The business situation

Illustrative reference architecture. Validate requirements for your organization.

Illustrative architecture: A newsroom prepares a feature that combines interviews, public records, photographs, and syndicated material. Editors need to know which claims are supported and which assets can be used in the planned channels. Agents organize evidence and draft checks; editors retain publication judgment and accountability.

Problem

A fluent draft can turn an allegation into a fact, misattribute a quotation, or apply rights granted for one channel to another. Web pages and source documents may also contain malicious instructions intended to redirect research tools.

Why a multi-agent system?

Claim verification, quotation alignment, rights review, and editorial assembly specialists inspect different evidence. The workflow preserves the exact source passages and licensed asset versions rather than relying on a general model's confidence.

Intended outcome

The intended result is a reviewable operational decision with source evidence and explicit ownership. Measure editorial check coverage and correction burden rather than equating generated volume with quality. Publication decisions depend on editorial standards and human judgment, with the system supplying a clearer record of supporting evidence and unresolved issues.

Architecture diagrams

Runtime flow

  1. 1

    Freeze draft revision

    Register the article version, planned channels, intended publication time, and accountable editor.

  2. 2

    Extract checkable claims

    Identify factual assertions and quotations while preserving their surrounding context and distinction from opinion.

  3. 3

    Gather source evidence

    Retrieve approved sources with timestamps and archived references; treat all retrieved instructions as untrusted content.

  4. 4

    Check asset permissions

    Compare planned channels, territories, and dates with recorded license metadata and surface missing approvals.

  5. 5

    Present editorial exceptions

    Show unsupported claims, attribution mismatches, contested evidence, and rights gaps for editor and specialist review.

  6. 6

    Release approved manifest

    Bind publication approval to the reviewed article and asset versions; any substantive edit reopens affected checks before publishing.

Failure recovery

A late edit changes a verified quotation.

Detect: Draft diff no longer matches the transcript span in the evidence manifest.

Recover: Invalidate the quotation check and require re-alignment before the editor can release the revision.

An image license excludes the planned social channel.

Detect: Usage metadata fails the channel-specific publication policy.

Recover: Block that asset for the channel and request an approved replacement or rights review.

Security & decision boundaries

  • Keep original source context and quotation timecodes accessible to editors.
  • Separate allegations, opinions, and verified facts in evidence labels.
  • Treat external pages and documents as untrusted data with no tool authority.
  • Require editor approval bound to article revision, assets, and distribution channels.
  • Route ambiguous rights interpretation to qualified reviewers without asserting legal clearance.

Operational metrics

Measurement definitions, not promised improvements.

Claim support coverage

Checkable factual claims with inspectable supporting evidence divided by claims requiring verification.

Quotation alignment accuracy

Audited quotations matching approved wording and attribution divided by audited quotations.

Rights exception closure

Required asset usage checks resolved before release divided by required checks.

Revision recheck latency

Time from substantive draft change to refreshed affected verification results.
