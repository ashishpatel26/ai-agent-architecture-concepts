Construction · INDUSTRY BLUEPRINT

Design change and site impact coordination

Trace a design revision through quantities, procurement, and site sequencing before project teams approve a change.

Explore in the architecture studio

The business situation

Illustrative reference architecture. Validate requirements for your organization.

Illustrative architecture: A commercial building project receives a revised mechanical layout after installation has begun. The contractor needs to identify affected work packages, material orders, and coordination questions. Agents prepare change evidence and scenarios; licensed designers and authorized project managers retain technical and commercial decisions.

Problem

Drawings, building models, requests for information, and purchase orders use different identifiers and revision conventions. A generated summary can overlook installed work or treat an unapproved sketch as the governing design, creating rework and contractual confusion.

Why a multi-agent system?

Revision, quantity, schedule, and procurement specialists examine distinct dependencies. Model comparison and quantity calculation services provide deterministic evidence while agents draft questions and explain bounded schedule scenarios.

Intended outcome

The intended result is a reviewable operational decision with source evidence and explicit ownership. Evaluate review preparation and missed dependencies before claiming reduced project delay. Construction outcomes depend on design resolution, site execution, and commercial agreement; the agent system should expose those dependencies rather than obscure them.

Architecture diagrams

Runtime flow

  1. 1

    Register issued revision

    Verify the document's issue status, project scope, and superseded revision before opening a change investigation.

  2. 2

    Compare design elements

    Run governed model or drawing comparisons and retain element identifiers and view references for human inspection.

  3. 3

    Map physical progress

    Join changed elements to installed quantities and active work packages, flagging unverified site reports.

  4. 4

    Evaluate consequences

    Calculate quantity deltas and bounded schedule scenarios, separating confirmed procurement commitments from estimates.

  5. 5

    Prepare coordinated review

    Present design questions, site conflicts, affected orders, and scenario assumptions to design and project-control owners.

  6. 6

    Publish authorized actions

    Record approved instructions through established project channels and track acknowledgement by each affected subcontractor or supplier.

Failure recovery

A draft sketch is mistaken for an issued revision.

Detect: Document status and transmittal checks show it lacks approved issue authority.

Recover: Quarantine it as discussion evidence and prevent downstream instruction generation.

Site progress reports conflict with installed-work photographs.

Detect: Element timestamps and field verification records disagree.

Recover: Request a current field check and keep quantity and schedule consequences provisional.

Security & decision boundaries

  • Require issued-document status and revision lineage before treating design information as authoritative.
  • Preserve licensed designer approval for technical design and safety decisions.
  • Keep contract notices and commercial commitments behind project-manager authorization.
  • Record quantity units and model element identifiers for every calculated delta.
  • Enforce project and subcontract access boundaries in drawings and cost records.

Operational metrics

Measurement definitions, not promised improvements.

Impact packet latency

Time from accepted revision to a coordinated review packet with affected work references.

Element traceability

Reported impacts linked to governed model or drawing elements divided by reported impacts.

Field verification coverage

Affected installed-work items supported by current verified site evidence divided by affected installed items.

Acknowledgement completeness

Approved instructions acknowledged by every required recipient divided by issued instruction sets.
