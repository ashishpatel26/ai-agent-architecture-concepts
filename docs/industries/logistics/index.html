Shipment exception recovery desk

Coordinate carrier evidence, dock capacity, and customer commitments for dispatcher-approved shipment recovery.

Explore in the architecture studio

The business situation

Illustrative reference architecture. Validate requirements for your organization.

Illustrative architecture: A third-party logistics operator manages delayed inbound loads to a distribution center. Carrier position feeds, driver messages, dock appointments, and customer delivery windows disagree. Dispatchers need feasible recovery options before a delay propagates into missed appointments and avoidable detention.

Problem

Position estimates do not establish arrival, and driver messages cannot authorize a new commercial commitment. Rebooking one dock slot may strand another load. An agent workflow must reconcile operational constraints without interfering with driver safety or carrier control.

Why a multi-agent system?

Tracking, appointment, customer-commitment, and recovery specialists investigate separate systems. A scheduling service evaluates feasible windows while agents explain uncertainty and coordinate a dispatcher-owned exception case.

Intended outcome

The intended result is a reviewable operational decision with source evidence and explicit ownership. Measure actionable recovery preparation and confirmed appointment outcomes. Delivery performance also depends on traffic, carrier execution, and facility operations, so improvements must be evaluated against comparable lanes rather than assumed.

Architecture diagrams

Runtime flow

  1. 1

    Capture delay signal

    Deduplicate carrier events and attach load, leg, event time, and expected customer window.

  2. 2

    Check tracking quality

    Classify observations as confirmed scans, carrier estimates, or stale positions before calculating available recovery time.

  3. 3

    Gather constraints

    Read dock capacity, site restrictions, order commitments, and permitted carrier options from authoritative systems.

  4. 4

    Evaluate options

    Run route and appointment feasibility checks; show assumptions about arrival and unloading duration explicitly.

  5. 5

    Obtain dispatcher decision

    Present costs, affected parties, and unresolved conditions; communication drafts stay pending until the dispatcher approves them.

  6. 6

    Commit coordinated changes

    Reserve the selected appointment, submit approved transport changes, and reconcile each acknowledgement before announcing a confirmed recovery.

Failure recovery

The position feed reports an impossible jump.

Detect: Speed and event-order checks conflict with previous trusted scans.

Recover: Discard the suspect estimate, retain the last confirmed event, and request carrier verification.

Dock rebooking succeeds but carrier acceptance fails.

Detect: The workflow has a booking receipt without a corresponding carrier acknowledgement.

Recover: Hold the recovery as partial and release or retain the slot according to the dispatcher's approved compensation policy.

Security & decision boundaries

  • Keep driver safety and legal driving constraints in validated planning services and human dispatch procedures.
  • Distinguish estimated arrival from carrier-confirmed arrival in every output.
  • Bind changes to load, leg, carrier, appointment, and source revision.
  • Restrict customer contract retrieval by account assignment and operating region.
  • Track appointment reservations with expiry and compensating release actions.

Operational metrics

Measurement definitions, not promised improvements.

Exception preparation time

Time from reliable delay signal to dispatcher-reviewable recovery options.

Arrival evidence freshness

Age of the latest trusted tracking event when a recovery decision is presented.

Recovery acknowledgement rate

Approved plans with every required carrier and facility receipt divided by approved plans.

Reservation leakage

Expired or abandoned dock reservations remaining active beyond their release deadline.
