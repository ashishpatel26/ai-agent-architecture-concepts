Energy · INDUSTRY BLUEPRINT

Renewable asset maintenance planning

Combine turbine condition evidence, weather windows, and spare-part availability into engineer-approved maintenance plans.

Explore in the architecture studio

The business situation

Illustrative reference architecture. Validate requirements for your organization.

Illustrative architecture: A wind-farm operator receives vibration anomalies from several turbines before a forecast weather change. Maintenance planners must decide which inspections are feasible given crew skills, access restrictions, parts, and production commitments. Agents support planning; turbine protection and dispatch control remain outside their authority.

Problem

An anomaly score is not a diagnosis, and forecast confidence changes with lead time. Recommending work from stale weather or incomplete maintenance history can waste a crew mobilization or introduce unsafe access assumptions.

Why a multi-agent system?

Condition, history, weather, and resource specialists gather independent evidence. A validated scheduling service applies access and crew constraints; engineers evaluate hypotheses and approve work scope through the maintenance system.

Intended outcome

The intended result is a reviewable operational decision with source evidence and explicit ownership. Measure planning effort and evidence completeness before claiming availability improvements. Actual reliability depends on engineering diagnosis, field execution, and equipment behavior; retain those factors separately in the operational record.

Architecture diagrams

Runtime flow

  1. 1

    Register anomaly

    Attach asset identity, sensor quality, detection interval, and existing work-order references to prevent duplicate investigations.

  2. 2

    Inspect condition evidence

    Compare the anomaly with operating regime and historical baseline without translating a statistical alert into a confirmed fault.

  3. 3

    Retrieve maintenance context

    Gather component age, recent interventions, unresolved inspections, and applicable procedure revisions.

  4. 4

    Evaluate feasible windows

    Apply current weather, access, crew, and parts constraints through a deterministic scheduling service with explicit forecast expiry.

  5. 5

    Review work package

    Present engineers with hypotheses, required inspections, mobilization dependencies, and conditions that would invalidate the plan.

  6. 6

    Publish approved work

    Reserve resources and create the approved work order; revalidate weather and access before mobilization through established operational procedures.

Failure recovery

A sensor calibration fault mimics equipment degradation.

Detect: Quality flags and neighboring sensor trends contradict the apparent anomaly.

Recover: Route a sensor-validation task and keep equipment conclusions provisional pending engineering review.

Weather conditions change after the work package is approved.

Detect: Forecast revision breaches the approved access window or plan expiry.

Recover: Suspend mobilization and return the plan to the authorized maintenance and safety owners.

Security & decision boundaries

  • Keep turbine control, protection, and market dispatch interfaces inaccessible to agents.
  • Use approved safety constraints from controlled procedures, never model-generated thresholds.
  • Attach sensor units, quality flags, and operating regime to every condition summary.
  • Bind work approval to equipment, procedure revision, resources, and weather assumptions.
  • Require current crew qualification and access authorization before mobilization.

Operational metrics

Measurement definitions, not promised improvements.

Work-package preparation time

Elapsed time from accepted anomaly to engineer-reviewable maintenance scope.

Evidence sufficiency

Work packages containing required sensor, history, and procedure references divided by reviewed packages.

Window invalidation rate

Approved plans suspended by changed access conditions divided by approved plans.

Resource reservation waste

Expired or cancelled reservations that were not released within the operational deadline.
