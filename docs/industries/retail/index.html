Retail · INDUSTRY BLUEPRINT

Store replenishment exception desk

Reconcile shelf observations, inventory records, and delivery plans into manager-approved replenishment actions.

Explore in the architecture studio

The business situation

Illustrative reference architecture. Validate requirements for your organization.

Illustrative architecture: A grocery chain sees apparent stockouts despite positive store inventory. Shelf photographs, point-of-sale activity, damaged-goods records, and backroom counts disagree. Store managers need a short task list that distinguishes a shelf gap from a receiving error or a genuinely unavailable item.

Problem

Inventory snapshots can lag sales and waste adjustments. Sending every discrepancy straight to purchasing creates excess stock, while treating shelf-image recognition as certain can dispatch staff to the wrong location or product variant.

Why a multi-agent system?

Specialists examine shelf evidence, ledger movements, inbound supply, and store task capacity. Their findings feed a deterministic inventory reconciliation and a bounded task proposal; agents do not independently alter counts or purchase stock.

Intended outcome

The intended result is a reviewable operational decision with source evidence and explicit ownership. Track useful staff checks and inventory corrections separately from sales. Improved shelf availability is an operational hypothesis to test against comparable stores, not an outcome guaranteed by adding agents.

Architecture diagrams

Runtime flow

  1. 1

    Register shelf gap

    Bind the observation to store, shelf, SKU candidates, and capture time; reject ambiguous location metadata.

  2. 2

    Reconcile movements

    Read the inventory watermark and all later sales or adjustments before explaining the discrepancy.

  3. 3

    Inspect alternatives

    Check backroom locations, substitute package identifiers, and scheduled inbound quantities while preserving uncertainty in product matches.

  4. 4

    Choose operational check

    Apply store rules to propose a shelf refill, physical count, receiving investigation, or monitored wait for inbound supply.

  5. 5

    Review manager task

    Show supporting evidence and expected staff effort; require explicit review for count adjustments or supplier-order changes.

  6. 6

    Close evidence loop

    Record the worker's scan or count, reconcile the ledger through authorized tools, and label the original hypothesis confirmed or rejected.

Failure recovery

Inventory feed lags a sales burst.

Detect: Movement watermark predates the shelf observation beyond the store's configured freshness limit.

Recover: Hold quantity-based recommendations and request a current scan or refreshed ledger snapshot.

Image matching confuses similar package sizes.

Detect: Product confidence and planogram identifier checks disagree.

Recover: Present both candidates and require a barcode scan before creating a SKU-specific task.

Security & decision boundaries

  • Use barcode or governed product identifiers before committing inventory changes.
  • Exclude shopper and employee identity from shelf-image processing where unnecessary.
  • Record pack-size conversions explicitly and calculate quantities deterministically.
  • Require manager approval for adjustments, orders, and substitution-policy changes.
  • Deduplicate tasks by store, shelf, SKU, and active investigation window.

Operational metrics

Measurement definitions, not promised improvements.

Shelf-gap validation

Reported shelf gaps confirmed by staff divided by shelf gaps investigated.

Task preparation latency

Time from valid observation to manager-ready task, excluding store execution delay.

Inventory freshness

Age of the latest reconciled movement watermark at recommendation time, reported by store.

Duplicate task rate

Tasks merged or rejected as duplicate work divided by proposed tasks.

Rollout plan

Recorded store scenarios

Managers validate pack conversions, stale inventory handling, and visually similar products using known examples.

Shadow task board

One store group compares proposed checks with actual staff findings and verifies image minimization.

Controlled task publishing

Managers enable approved assignments after duplicate-task and late-ledger-update exercises pass.
