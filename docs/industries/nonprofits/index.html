Grant delivery and beneficiary evidence desk

Coordinate restricted funding, program evidence, and reporting obligations while staff retain funding and beneficiary decisions.

Explore in the architecture studio

The business situation

Illustrative reference architecture. Validate requirements for your organization.

Illustrative architecture: A nonprofit runs community programs funded by several restricted grants. Program staff need to connect delivery records, approved budgets, and reporting requirements without exposing beneficiary information unnecessarily. Agents prepare grant evidence and exception packets; accountable staff approve spending, eligibility, and external reports.

Problem

The same activity can serve multiple programs but must not be counted twice in a grant report. Narrative records may contain sensitive personal circumstances, and donor restrictions can differ by award amendment. A fluent summary can hide missing evidence or unsupported impact claims.

Why a multi-agent system?

Grant-rule, expenditure, program-evidence, and reporting specialists work within funding and privacy boundaries. Deterministic allocation services reconcile amounts, while staff decide acceptable evidence and professional judgments about program delivery.

Intended outcome

The intended result is a reviewable operational decision with source evidence and explicit ownership. Measure staff preparation and reporting accuracy while preserving dignity and privacy. Social outcomes require separate evaluation design and evidence; the agent workflow should not convert activity counts into invented claims of impact.

Runtime flow

  1. 1

    Open reporting period

    Bind the packet to grant, reporting interval, award amendment version, and accountable program owner.

  2. 2

    Retrieve requirements

    Map approved reporting obligations and budget restrictions without assuming terms carry across different awards.

  3. 3

    Reconcile expenditure

    Calculate eligible candidate allocations using deterministic rules and flag overlaps for finance review.

  4. 4

    Assemble delivery evidence

    Link activities to verified records, aggregate beneficiary information where possible, and retain missing evidence explicitly.

  5. 5

    Review report claims

    Present program and finance leaders with supported metrics, proposed narrative, unresolved restrictions, and any consent gaps.

  6. 6

    Submit approved record

    Publish only the reviewed report through the authorized workflow and preserve submission receipts and follow-up obligations.

Failure recovery

One activity is counted in two incompatible grant totals.

Detect: Cross-award allocation checks find duplicated activity identifiers beyond approved sharing rules.

Recover: Hold both affected report sections and ask program and finance owners to reconcile the allocation.

A beneficiary story lacks appropriate publication permission.

Detect: Consent metadata does not cover the intended audience or report channel.

Recover: Remove identifiable content and request an approved aggregate alternative or valid permission through staff procedures.

Security & decision boundaries

  • Keep beneficiary eligibility and service decisions with accountable program staff.
  • Separate observed delivery metrics from claimed social impact or causal conclusions.
  • Validate grant-specific allocation rules and prevent duplicate unsupported reporting.
  • Minimize beneficiary identity in model prompts and restrict original service records.
  • Require program and finance approval before spending commitments or donor submissions.

Operational metrics

Measurement definitions, not promised improvements.

Evidence coverage

Reported delivery metrics linked to verified activity records divided by reported metrics.

Allocation exception rate

Candidate expenditure lines requiring finance correction divided by lines reviewed.

Reporting preparation time

Time from period close to a leader-reviewable grant packet with unresolved items identified.

Consent coverage

Identifiable stories or assets with permission matching the intended use divided by proposed identifiable items.
