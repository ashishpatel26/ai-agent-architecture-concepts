Engagement delivery evidence coordinator

Connect client requirements, delivery evidence, staffing constraints, and commercial scope before engagement leaders commit changes.

Explore in the architecture studio

The business situation

Illustrative reference architecture. Validate requirements for your organization.

Illustrative architecture: A consulting team prepares a milestone review for a client transformation program. Deliverables are spread across workspaces, acceptance criteria live in a statement of work, and staffing changes affect the schedule. Agents assemble a review packet while engagement leaders retain client commitments and professional judgments.

Problem

A polished progress summary can treat an uploaded draft as an accepted deliverable or omit a scope change hidden in meeting notes. Reusing prior-client examples also risks confidentiality leakage when shared knowledge stores lack engagement boundaries.

Why a multi-agent system?

Scope, delivery-evidence, capacity, and briefing specialists inspect distinct systems. A milestone workflow separates contractual criteria, observed evidence, and proposed changes so leaders can make a concrete decision.

Intended outcome

The intended result is a reviewable operational decision with source evidence and explicit ownership. Measure preparation effort and unrecognized delivery gaps, not just report throughput. A useful assistant makes commitments and evidence easier to inspect while preserving professional accountability and client confidentiality.

Architecture diagrams

Runtime flow

  1. 1

    Open milestone review

    Bind the review to engagement, reporting date, contract revision, and accountable delivery leader.

  2. 2

    Collect acceptance criteria

    Retrieve signed scope and approved changes; retain unresolved commercial questions separately from operational requirements.

  3. 3

    Inspect delivery evidence

    Map each criterion to the exact artifact version and acceptance record, exposing missing or provisional evidence.

  4. 4

    Evaluate capacity constraints

    Run bounded schedule scenarios using actual availability and dependencies without assuming overtime or unapproved role substitutions.

  5. 5

    Prepare leader decision

    Show accepted work, remaining gaps, forecast assumptions, and proposed client questions for engagement-leader review.

  6. 6

    Record approved commitments

    Publish the reviewed report or change request through existing channels and track client acknowledgement independently of internal approval.

Failure recovery

An internal draft is mistaken for client acceptance.

Detect: Acceptance registry lacks the required client signoff for the artifact version.

Recover: Mark the milestone unaccepted and request the missing review without rewriting the historical status.

A retrieved example belongs to a restricted prior engagement.

Detect: Engagement access policy rejects the document's client and purpose attributes.

Recover: Exclude it and use an approved reusable asset or independently authored example.

Security & decision boundaries

  • Enforce client and engagement isolation across retrieval, caches, and report exports.
  • Distinguish draft, internally reviewed, client-submitted, and client-accepted deliverables.
  • Bind commercial commitments to signed scope and approved change records.
  • Require leader approval before client communications, staffing assignments, or schedule promises.
  • Exclude confidential personnel and billing details from broad delivery summaries.

Operational metrics

Measurement definitions, not promised improvements.

Acceptance evidence coverage

Milestone criteria linked to valid acceptance evidence divided by criteria claimed complete.

Review preparation time

Time from reporting cutoff to leader-reviewable milestone packet.

Forecast revision rate

Published forecasts changed after unrecorded capacity or scope facts emerge divided by published forecasts.

Confidentiality filter quality

Authorized reusable assets correctly available and restricted client assets correctly denied in a curated evaluation.
