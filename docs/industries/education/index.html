Student support case coordination

Coordinate academic records, support resources, and appointment options while staff retain consequential student decisions.

Explore in the architecture studio

The business situation

Illustrative reference architecture. Validate requirements for your organization.

Illustrative architecture: A university support office receives a student's request involving course registration, an approaching deadline, and difficulty accessing campus services. Advisers need a current view of relevant records and available support. Agents prepare options and referrals without determining admissions, discipline, disability eligibility, or academic outcomes.

Problem

Support requests span systems with different permissions and sensitive personal information. Inferring a disability or risk category from conversational text can create harmful labels. Outdated course rules can also lead to an incorrect promise about progression or fees.

Why a multi-agent system?

Academic-record, policy, resource, and case-coordination specialists retrieve only the information needed for the student's stated request. The workflow distinguishes official academic decisions from assistance and leaves sensitive disclosures under explicit access controls.

Intended outcome

The intended result is a reviewable operational decision with source evidence and explicit ownership. Evaluate whether students reach appropriate staff with fewer repeated explanations while protecting their information. Faster answers are insufficient if policy applicability, referral acceptance, or the student's stated preferences are lost.

Architecture diagrams

Runtime flow

  1. 1

    Authenticate request

    Verify student identity and capture the specific assistance requested without requiring unrelated sensitive disclosures.

  2. 2

    Bind permitted context

    Resolve adviser role, program scope, and purpose before retrieving academic or protected support records.

  3. 3

    Retrieve current rules

    Match program and term to effective deadlines and policies, exposing ambiguity instead of promising an exception.

  4. 4

    Find suitable resources

    Query available services against explicit preferences, access requirements, and scheduling constraints.

  5. 5

    Prepare adviser review

    Show official facts, cited rules, unanswered questions, and optional referrals without inferring student traits or outcomes.

  6. 6

    Record agreed follow-up

    Save approved appointments or tasks with student consent where required and track whether a responsible staff member accepts the handoff.

Failure recovery

A policy search returns last year's deadline.

Detect: Term and effective-date checks conflict with the student's current registration period.

Recover: Reject the stale rule and route unresolved deadline questions to the registrar or adviser.

A case summary includes unrelated protected notes.

Detect: Purpose and field-level access validation flags information outside the request scope.

Recover: Remove the material, record the access incident for authorized review, and rebuild the packet with minimum necessary context.

Security & decision boundaries

  • Apply purpose-specific access to academic, disability, financial, and counseling records.
  • Do not infer sensitive traits or assign risk labels from ordinary support conversations.
  • Keep admissions, grades, discipline, and eligibility decisions with authorized staff.
  • Display term and program applicability for every policy citation.
  • Require an accepted staff handoff for urgent or unresolved support requests.

Operational metrics

Measurement definitions, not promised improvements.

Policy applicability

Audited citations matching the student's program and term divided by audited citations.

Case preparation time

Elapsed time from authenticated request to adviser-reviewable options and outstanding questions.

Handoff acceptance

Referred cases accepted by the responsible service within its defined window divided by referred cases.

Unnecessary data exposure

Audited case packets containing out-of-purpose information divided by audited packets.
