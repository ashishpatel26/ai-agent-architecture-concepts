40 — Explainable AI (XAI)

Provide audience-appropriate explanations grounded in actual evidence, rules, and execution records without presenting generated rationales as faithful internal reasoning.

Concept overview

Explainable AI helps people understand a system's outputs, relevant evidence, operating limits, and appropriate use. In agent systems, explanations can show retrieved sources, tool observations, applicable rules, decision factors, and unresolved uncertainty. The explanation should match the user's task: an operator diagnosing a failure needs different detail from a customer contesting an outcome.

A language model can generate a plausible rationale that does not faithfully describe how its output was produced. Do not equate that rationale with causal explanation or private reasoning. Build explanations from recorded facts and validated decision logic, and assess whether they are accurate, understandable, and useful for the intended audience.

Why it matters in production

Proof of concept

A prototype can expose citations and simple decision factors, then ask users whether those help them detect incorrect outputs.

Production

Production separates factual evidence, deterministic rules, model judgments, and limitations. Preserve the records needed to support an explanation and provide a route to correction or review.

Enterprise scale

At scale, evaluate explanation fidelity and usefulness by audience and language. Avoid exposing sensitive attributes, confidential source material, or exploitable security details in the name of transparency.

Architecture components

Decision evidence store

Retains relevant inputs, sources, tool outcomes, and rule results for the actual execution.

Explanation builder

Produces structured reasons from recorded evidence rather than invented retrospective narratives.

Audience policy

Selects the detail and data that the recipient is authorized and able to use.

Fidelity evaluator

Checks whether explanations accurately represent supported factors and known limitations.

Review interface

Lets users inspect evidence, challenge errors, and request accountable reconsideration.

The architecture, mapped

Runtime flow

  1. 1

    Identify audience need

    Determine whether the explanation supports understanding, debugging, review, or contesting an outcome.

  2. 2

    Collect actual evidence

    Load the sources, rule results, and execution records associated with the decision.

  3. 3

    Separate factor types

    Distinguish verified facts, deterministic conditions, model judgments, and unresolved uncertainty.

  4. 4

    Construct explanation

    Summarize supported factors with source links and appropriate detail.

  5. 5

    Validate fidelity and access

    Check the explanation against records and remove unauthorized information.

  6. 6

    Enable response

    Provide a correction or review path and measure whether users can act on the explanation.

Explaining a claim document requirement

Insurance operations · Illustrative enterprise scenario, not a reported customer outcome.

Business situation

An insurer's assistant identifies additional documents needed to complete a commercial claim review.

The problem

Customers receive vague requests and cannot tell whether the requirement comes from policy wording, missing evidence, or a model inference.

Agent requirement

The assistant must explain each requested document using the actual case record and approved requirements.

Architecture

An explanation builder combines missing-field checks, policy references, and reviewer notes with an audience-specific disclosure policy.

Runtime

  • The system identifies a missing repair estimate and cites the corresponding case requirement and relevant policy reference.
  • The customer sees what is missing, why it is requested, and how to correct an error; staff can inspect the underlying execution evidence.

Failure scenario

If a reason cannot be linked to a verified requirement, it is not presented as authoritative and the case is routed for review.

Operational impact

Measure explanation accuracy, customer correction success, reviewer usefulness, and unauthorized-detail exposure. An understandable explanation does not by itself validate the underlying decision.

Architecture tradeoffs

DecisionBenefitCost
Detailed evidenceUsers can inspect and challenge specific claims.Excess detail can overwhelm or expose restricted information.
Generated summariesComplex records become easier to understand.Fluency can conceal unsupported causal stories.
Audience-specific viewsExplanations become more useful and appropriately scoped.Multiple views require consistent source-of-truth management.

Failure scenarios & recovery

A rationale names a factor never used

Detect: Fidelity checks find no supporting execution record.

Recover: Remove the claim and explain only recorded evidence.

An explanation reveals another party's private data

Detect: Access validation identifies unauthorized source detail.

Recover: Provide a permitted summary or review route without disclosure.

Common architecture mistakes

Requesting a persuasive post-hoc rationale

Construct reasons from actual evidence and rules.

Equating feature importance with causation

State the method's limits and validate interpretation.

Using one explanation for every audience

Design views around concrete user needs.

When to choose this pattern

Choose it when

  • Users need to understand or challenge consequential outputs.
  • Operators require evidence to diagnose agent behavior.
  • Actual decision records can support faithful explanations.

Avoid it when

  • The explanation would be invented without supporting records.
  • Disclosure would expose unauthorized private information.
  • A persuasive narrative is being used to hide uncertainty.

Architecture checklist

  • Define audience and explanation purpose.
  • Retain actual decision evidence.
  • Separate facts, rules, and judgments.
  • Validate explanation fidelity.
  • Apply source-level access controls.
  • Provide correction and review mechanisms.
